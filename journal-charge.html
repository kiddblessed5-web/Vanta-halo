Inside the fourteen-minute charge

Technology · 6 min read

Most fast chargers promise a headline number and then spend the rest of the session apologising for it. The rate drops, the cells heat up and you wait. The Halo is built around the opposite idea: keep the power high and the heat low.

Our solid-state cells replace the liquid electrolyte with a ceramic layer. It conducts well and does not catch fire, but the real benefit is thermal. The cells can accept a high current for longer before the pack needs to slow down.

On a 320 kW charger, the car reaches its peak within two minutes and holds above 290 kW until about the fifth. By minute fourteen, the battery goes from 10 to 80 per cent. That is roughly the time it takes to order a coffee and drink half of it.

A fast charger is only as fast as the battery lets it be.

For the Nairobi to Mombasa run, that changes the plan. One stop at the midpoint is enough, and the car pre-conditions its battery on the way, so you arrive at the charger ready to take full power.

At home, the included 22 kW wall box adds more than 400 km overnight. Most owners will visit a public charger a handful of times a year.
